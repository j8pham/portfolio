Key Decision Point: Day 3

During ideation, I proposed the drag-and-drop timeline concept. The team debated between three approaches:

Option A
Wizard flow

Step-by-step questions. Safe but boring.

Option B ✓
Visual timeline

Drag goals to ages. Tangible, memorable.

Option C
Chat interface

Conversational. High dev effort for sprint.

Why I pushed for Option B: It made the abstract (future planning) feel concrete. Users could literally see their future on a timeline. The interaction itself communicated "you're in control."

Act III: The Solution

A planner that speaks human

We built Secure Your Future—a tool that lets you pick the life moments you care about, see them on a timeline, and get a plan that actually makes sense. When you're ready to talk to someone, they already know your story.

Step 1

A welcome that feels like a friend

  • No forms on the first screen
  • Mountain imagery = journey, not transaction
  • One button: "Get Started"

The first screen sets the tone. We wanted it to feel like opening a door, not filling out paperwork.

Why This Approach?

Q:

Why a single CTA instead of multiple entry points?
In testing, users felt overwhelmed by competitor sites with 6+ options on the landing page. One button reduced decision paralysis and increased click-through by 40% vs. our multi-CTA variant.

Q:

Why the mountain imagery?
Mountains signal a journey with a destination—growth, not transaction. 4 of 5 test participants used words like "hopeful" or "calm" to describe this page vs. "anxious" for traditional financial sites.

Step 2

Pick the milestones that matter to you

House? Family? Retirement? Travel? You choose what's important. The tool adapts to you, not the other way around.

  • ✓ Select up to three life goals
  • ✓ Each choice changes what comes next
  • ✓ No wrong answers
Step 3

See your future on a timeline

Drag your goals onto a timeline. See when you want things to happen. Suddenly the future isn't so abstract—it's a plan you can actually picture.

  • ✓ Drag and drop to arrange
  • ✓ Goals map to ages/timeframes
  • ✓ Edit anytime—it's your plan

Decisions & Trade-offs: Timeline Builder

Q:

Why drag-and-drop instead of dropdowns or a form?
Dropdowns feel like work; drag-and-drop feels like play. Users described the timeline as "fun" and "visual"—emotions rarely associated with financial planning. The interaction model itself became a differentiator.

Q:

What about accessibility for drag-and-drop?
This is a real trade-off. We spec'd keyboard alternatives (arrow keys + Enter) but didn't implement them in the sprint. Given more time, I'd prioritize ARIA live regions and keyboard navigation.

Q:

Why limit to 3 goals?
Research showed users with 4+ goals felt overwhelmed. Three felt manageable without feeling restrictive. We considered letting users add more later (progressive disclosure) but scoped it out for the sprint.

Step 4

Get a plan—and a real conversation

Your summary shows what you care about. When you book an advisor, they see it all—so the call isn't "tell me about yourself" but "let's talk about buying that house."

  • ✓ Clear summary of your goals
  • ✓ One tap to book an advisor
  • ✓ They already know your story
The Details

Built on Co-operators' design system

This wasn't a blue-sky concept—we built it to fit right into Co-operators' existing brand. Same colors, same typography, ready for real development.

How We Measured This

+65% Qualified Leads

Comparative estimate based on advisor feedback. Advisors reviewed our prototype's output (goal context, timeline, priorities) vs. current form submissions. 3 advisors rated our leads as "significantly more actionable" in blind comparison.

100% Task Completion

In moderated usability testing with 6 participants (ages 24–58), all 6 successfully completed the primary task: "Select your life goals, place them on a timeline, and book an advisor." Compared to our benchmark of 40% completion on Co-operators' existing form-based flow.

5 Pilot Teams

Post-presentation, 5 Co-operators regional teams expressed interest in piloting the concept with real users.

Note: These are sprint-week validation metrics, not production data. A proper A/B test with statistical significance would be the next step.

Biggest Unvalidated Assumption
